# What Is Statistics?

**Statistics** is the science of collecting, organizing, summarizing, analyzing, interpreting, and communicating data. It helps analysts identify patterns and make decisions under uncertainty.

## Main Types of Statistics

- **Descriptive statistics:** Summarizes observed data.
- **Inferential statistics:** Uses a sample to draw conclusions about a population.
- **Exploratory data analysis:** Searches for patterns, relationships, and unusual values.
- **Predictive analytics:** Uses historical data and models to estimate future outcomes.

## Important Statistical Terms

- **Population:** Entire group being studied.
- **Sample:** Smaller group selected from the population.
- **Variable:** A characteristic that can have different values.
- **Observation:** One recorded case or row.
- **Categorical data:** Labels or groups such as region or product type.
- **Numerical data:** Quantities that can be measured or counted.
- **Mean:** Sum of values divided by the number of values.
- **Median:** Middle value after sorting.
- **Mode:** Most frequently occurring value.
- **Range:** Maximum minus minimum.
- **Variance:** Average squared distance from the mean.
- **Standard deviation:** Typical distance of values from the mean.
- **Percentile:** Value below which a given percentage of observations falls.
- **Quartile:** A value dividing ordered data into four parts.
- **Outlier:** An unusually distant observation.
- **Correlation:** Strength and direction of association between variables.
- **Causation:** A relationship where one factor produces a change in another.
- **Probability:** Numerical measure of how likely an event is.
- **Hypothesis:** Testable statement about a population or relationship.
- **Null hypothesis:** Statement that there is no effect or relationship.
- **Alternative hypothesis:** Statement that an effect or relationship exists.
- **Confidence interval:** Range of plausible values for a population estimate.
- **Significance level:** Chosen threshold for evaluating evidence against the null hypothesis.
- **P-value:** Probability of results at least as extreme as the observed results if the null hypothesis is true.
- **Bias:** Systematic error that makes results unrepresentative.
- **Sampling:** Process of selecting observations from a population.

## Common Statistical Methods

- Frequency tables and cross-tabulation.
- Mean, median, variance, and standard deviation.
- Percentages, ratios, and growth rates.
- Correlation and regression.
- Hypothesis tests and confidence intervals.
- Time-series analysis and forecasting.
- A/B testing and experiment analysis.

## Use of Statistics in Data Analytics

Statistics helps analysts summarize sales, compare customer groups, measure campaign performance, detect unusual transactions, estimate demand, evaluate experiments, and communicate uncertainty. For example, an analyst can compare the average order value of two customer segments and use a confidence interval to describe the estimate.

## Important Cautions

Correlation does not prove causation. A biased sample, missing data, incorrect measurement, or misleading chart can produce unreliable conclusions. Analysts should inspect data quality, explain assumptions, use suitable methods, and avoid overstating results.

## Conclusion

Statistics gives data analytics a reliable foundation for describing data, testing ideas, predicting outcomes, and making evidence-based decisions.
