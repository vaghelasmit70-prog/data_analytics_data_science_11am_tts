# What Is Agile Methodology?

**Agile methodology** is an iterative approach to project and software development. Teams deliver work in small increments, collect feedback, adapt to change, and focus on customer value.

## Important Agile Terms

- **Agile Manifesto:** Values individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration, and responding to change.
- **Iteration:** A repeated development cycle that produces an improved result.
- **Increment:** A usable piece of the product delivered during an iteration.
- **Product owner:** Represents customer needs and prioritizes the product work.
- **Scrum master:** Helps the team follow Scrum and removes process obstacles.
- **Development team:** Creates, tests, and delivers the increment.
- **Stakeholder:** A person or group affected by or interested in the project.
- **Product backlog:** Ordered list of desired features, fixes, and improvements.
- **Sprint backlog:** Work selected from the product backlog for one sprint.
- **User story:** A short requirement written from the user's point of view.
- **Acceptance criteria:** Conditions that must be met for a story to be accepted.
- **Epic:** A large requirement that is divided into smaller user stories.
- **Task:** A specific piece of work needed to complete a story.
- **Definition of Done:** Shared quality conditions that work must satisfy.
- **Sprint:** A time-boxed period in Scrum, often one to four weeks.
- **Daily Scrum:** A short daily event for coordination and planning.
- **Sprint planning:** Meeting where the team selects and plans sprint work.
- **Sprint review:** Session where the team demonstrates the increment and receives feedback.
- **Sprint retrospective:** Meeting to discuss what went well and what to improve.
- **Kanban board:** Visualizes work as cards moving through stages such as To Do, Doing, and Done.
- **Work in progress limit:** Restricts the amount of unfinished work.
- **Velocity:** Amount of work a team completes in an iteration; it is a planning aid, not a personal performance score.
- **Burndown chart:** Shows remaining work over time.
- **Backlog refinement:** Clarifies, estimates, and reorders upcoming work.

## Agile in Data Analytics

An analytics team can deliver a first dashboard with essential metrics, gather stakeholder feedback, and improve it in later iterations. Agile supports changing business questions, incremental data pipelines, regular data-quality checks, and early delivery of useful insights.

## Benefits and Challenges

Agile improves feedback, visibility, adaptability, and early value. It requires active stakeholder participation, disciplined prioritization, clear acceptance criteria, and a team that can communicate effectively. It does not mean skipping documentation, testing, or planning.

## Conclusion

Agile is a flexible way to deliver valuable work through short cycles and continuous learning. It is useful for analytics projects because requirements and insights often change as data is explored.
